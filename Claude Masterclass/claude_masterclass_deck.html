Foundations

What Is a Large Language Model?

A model trained to predict the next word — and the next, and the next — until a full, coherent answer emerges.

In practice

Prompt: “The capital of France is”

Model predicts: “Paris” — the highest-probability next word, based on everything it saw in training.

It isn’t a search engine and it isn’t a database — it’s pattern completion, at a massive scale.

01

Data

The text, code, and images it learned from

02

Parameters

The learned weights — its “neurons”

03

The raw processing power used to train it

Each dimension has grown by orders of magnitude every few years — that growth is what unlocked modern LLM capability.

Foundations · Large #1

The Data

Common Crawl

Petabytes of raw, periodic web crawls — the backbone of most pretraining corpora

 FineWeb (Hugging Face)

A cleaned, deduplicated ~15-trillion-token web dataset distilled from Common Crawl

 The Stack v2 (BigCode)

Permissively-licensed source code across hundreds of programming languages

 The Pile (EleutherAI)

An 800GB curated mix of books, papers, code, and web text for language modeling

Quality now matters as much as volume — labs filter and de-duplicate rather than just scraping more. Click any card to open the dataset’s own page.

Foundations · Large #2

The Parameters

Parameters are the learned connection strengths between artificial “neurons” — the model’s knobs, tuned during training.

EraModelRoughly
2019GPT-2~1.5 billion parameters
2020GPT-3~175 billion parameters
2023GPT-4 & Claude-classUndisclosed — estimated hundreds of billions+
2024–25DeepSeek-V3 & peers671B total / 37B active per token (Mixture-of-Experts)

Labs increasingly stop publishing exact counts — parameter count alone no longer predicts quality. Click a model name to open its paper or page and verify the numbers yourself.

Foundations · Large #3

The Compute

Training a frontier model means running thousands of GPUs or TPUs for weeks to months — a single run can cost tens of millions of dollars.

Hardware

Clusters of thousands of specialized GPU/TPU chips, networked together

Time

Weeks to months of continuous, uninterrupted training

Energy

Megawatt-scale power draw — comparable to a small town

Cost

$10M–$100M+ per frontier training run

This is why only a handful of companies can train frontier models from scratch.

Foundations

Not All Language Models Are Built the Same

Dense

Every parameter activates on every request — simple and predictable

Mixture of Experts

Only a few specialized “expert” sub-networks activate per request

Reasoning models

Trained to “think” step-by-step before answering hard problems

Foundations

Three Families, Three Examples

TypeDefinitionExample
DenseEvery parameter used on every request; simplest, most predictable costClaude Sonnet/Haiku family, Meta Llama 3

ReasoningGenerates extra “thinking” tokens before the final answer — stronger on math/logic, slower & costlierClaude Extended Thinking, OpenAI o-series, DeepSeek-R1

Foundations

How LLM Cost Is Actually Measured

LLMs don’t charge per question — they charge per token, the small chunks of text sent in and returned.

Rule of thumb

1 token ≈ ¾ of an English word. “Leadership” might be 2 tokens; “the” is 1 token.

Project instructions — paste this once, into the Project's instructions

You are the resume-database assistant for HR. You work strictly from the resumes attached to this Project — never from general knowledge or assumptions about a candidate. You handle two kinds of requests: 1. Screening against a role — when given a job description and must-have criteria, evaluate every resume and return a verdict for each candidate: Strong Match, Partial Match, or No Match, with a one-line justification. 2. A Plugin is an installable bundle that packages one app's commands together — install it once, and its slash commands just work.

Why it matters

It turns a whole external app into something you control with one typed command, right inside your chat.

  1. 1. Install the plugin once (e.g. claude plugins add canva)
  2. 2. Connect the account it needs, if any (e.g. your Canva login)
  3. 3. Use its slash commands directly, right in the chat
