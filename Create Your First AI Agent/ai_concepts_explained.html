1Family A · What the AI does

Generative AI

AI that creates something new (text, images, code, audio) when you ask it to.

🍳

In the kitchen

A brilliant chef who can describe any recipe from memory, write you a menu or invent a dish on paper. But the chef never touches a stove. You still do the cooking.

💡

Everyday example

You ask for a polite email to reschedule a meeting and it writes one in seconds. You copy it, check it and press send.

2Family A · What the AI does

AI agent

An AI that does not only answer. It uses tools to carry out a task for you, step by step.

🍳

In the kitchen

The same chef, but now allowed into the pantry. They check the fridge, order what is missing and cook one dish for you, then ask if you like it.

"Book me the cheapest Friday flight to Milan." The agent opens airline sites, compares prices, fills in the form and stops to ask before paying.

3Family A · What the AI does

Agentic AI

A system that works toward a big goal on its own: it plans, shares the work between several agents and changes course when something goes wrong.

🍳

In the kitchen

The whole restaurant on a busy night: a head chef reads the orders, sous chefs, a pastry cook and a runner each take their part, and when the oven breaks the head chef re-plans.

💡

Everyday example

A car-insurance claim arrives. One AI reads the documents, others check the policy and look for fraud, one sends the payment. A coordinator keeps them on track. Nobody types a command in between.

4Family B · How we build and control it

Harness engineering

Designing everything around the AI model (its tools, rules, memory, permissions and safety checks) so it works safely and reliably.

🍳

In the kitchen

Same chef, better kitchen. Sharp knives in the right drawer, labelled shelves, hygiene rules, a "taste before you serve" check, and a manager who must approve any big purchase.

💡

Everyday example

A coding assistant that may only edit files in one project folder, asks before deleting anything, remembers your style and checks its work before showing you. The AI model is the engine. The harness is the car around it.

5Family B · How we build and control it

Loop engineering

Designing the try, check, fix, repeat cycle so the AI keeps improving its work until it is actually right.

🍳

In the kitchen

The chef tastes the soup, adds salt, tastes again, and only sends it out when it is right. Nobody trusts a first attempt.

💡

Everyday example

An AI writes a small program and runs 8 automatic tests. Two fail, so it fixes them and runs the tests again. It stops only when all 8 pass (or after a safe maximum number of tries).

6Family B · How we build and control it

Graph engineering

Designing the map of steps: who does what, in which order, and which road to take at each decision.

🍳

In the kitchen

The order flowchart on the kitchen wall. Order comes in, starters station, mains station. If the guest has an allergy, go to special prep. Then plating, then serve.

💡

Everyday example

A loan request. The AI reads the form and checks the credit score. Good score: approve automatically. Weak score: send to a human. Every route is drawn in advance, so the result is predictable and easy to audit.

Putting it together

One product, all six ideas

Real products do not pick one word. They stack all of them. Here is how a bank's AI customer-support helper uses each idea.

The product

A bank's AI support helper

A customer writes: "I was charged twice for the same payment." Watch which idea does which job.

Products like this exist today, for example Intercom Fin, Zendesk AI agents and Salesforce Agentforce.

1
Generative AI

Understands the customer's message and writes a friendly, clear reply.

2
AI agent

Looks up the account, finds the duplicate payment and starts a refund. It acts, not just talks.

3
Agentic AI

A triage agent, a billing agent and an escalation agent work together toward one goal: resolve this customer's problem.

4
Harness engineering

It may refund up to a set limit only, never shows another customer's data, and logs every action.

5
Loop engineering

After each fix it asks "Did that solve it?" and tries the next option until the customer says yes.

6
Graph engineering

The route is mapped: greet, identify, diagnose, then simple case goes to fix and complex case goes to a human.

Your turn

Which word fits?

Read each situation and tap the word that describes it best.

A fair warning about vocabulary. Words 1 to 3 are widely understood. Words 4 to 6 are newer industry vocabulary, and different teams use them a little differently. One product can also fit several words at once: Claude Code, for example, is an agent, has a harness, runs loops and can use sub-agents. The goal is not to label things perfectly, but to know which question each word helps you ask.
